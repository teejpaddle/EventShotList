The basics

Three settings control how bright a photo is. Each one also changes how the photo looks, so changing one means adjusting another to keep the same brightness.

  • Shutter speed

    How long the camera collects light for each photo.

    Fast (1/500 s) freezes movement. Slow (1/30 s) lets moving people and shaky hands blur.

    Slower = brighter

  • Aperture (f-number)

    How wide the opening in the lens is.

    A small number (f/2.8) lets in more light and blurs the background. A big number (f/8) keeps more of the scene sharp.

    Smaller number = brighter

  • ISO

    How much the camera boosts the light it receives.

    Higher ISO brightens the photo but adds grain, called noise. Use only as much as you need.

    Higher = brighter

Start in Aperture Priority with Auto ISO

You pick the aperture, which decides how much of the scene is sharp. You set a minimum shutter speed so moving people do not blur. The camera picks the ISO to make the photo bright enough.

Switch to Manual when the light stays the same but the camera keeps getting it wrong. Examples: a performer in a spotlight against a black stage, or a series of group photos that must all match. In Manual, you set shutter speed and aperture yourself, and Auto ISO can still handle brightness.

Starting settings

Set these once before the event. Each card says why, and what to change if the photos look wrong.

  • Shooting mode

    A (Aperture Priority)Av (Aperture-priority AE)A (Aperture-priority auto)

    Why: You control how much is in focus, and the camera handles brightness.

    If it looks wrong: Photos too bright or too dark? Use exposure compensation (below) before switching to Manual.

  • ISO

    Auto ISO, highest ISO about 12800

    Why: The camera brightens the photo in dim halls without slowing the shutter.

    If it looks wrong: Too grainy? Open the aperture (smaller f-number) or move closer to the light.

  • Minimum shutter speed

    1/250 s (1/125 s for a seated audience)

    Why: People move. Below this speed, hands, faces, and dancers smear.

    If it looks wrong: Still blurry? Raise it to 1/500 s. Too dark even at the highest ISO? Lower it to 1/125 s.

  • Focus mode

    Continuous AF (AF-C)Servo AFAF-C (Continuous AF)

    Why: Focus keeps adjusting while you half-press the shutter button, so people who move stay sharp.

    If it looks wrong: Focus lands on the background? Use a smaller focus area or turn on eye detection.

  • Focus area

    ZoneExpand AF area: AroundWide-area AF (L)

    Why: A medium-size area is easy to place on a person and rarely grabs the wrong thing.

    If it looks wrong: Camera picks the wrong person? Switch to a single small point and put it on your subject.

  • Face and eye detection

    Face/Eye Prior. in AF: On · Face/Eye Subject: HumanSubject to detect: People · Eye detection: AutoAF/MF subject detection options: People

    Why: The camera finds faces and eyes for you. Eyes are what must be sharp.

    If it looks wrong: Focus jumps between faces? Move the focus area over the person you want.

  • White balance

    Auto, with Priority Set in AWB: AmbienceAuto (Ambience priority)Keep overall atmosphere

    Why: Halls mix warm bulbs, LED panels, and daylight. Auto handles the mix, and this option keeps the warm glow of diyas.

    If it looks wrong: Skin looks too orange? Change Priority Set in AWB to White. RAW files can also be fixed later.Skin looks too orange? Switch to Auto (White priority). RAW files can also be fixed later.Skin looks too orange? Switch to Keep white (reduce warm colors). RAW files can also be fixed later.

  • File format

    RAW & JPEGRAW + JPEG LRAW + JPEG fine★

    Why: The JPEG is ready to share right away. The RAW keeps extra detail so an editor can rescue dark, bright, or orange photos.

    If it looks wrong: Card filling up too fast? Bring a bigger or second card rather than turning off RAW.

  • Drive mode (burst)

    Continuous Shooting: MidLow-speed continuousContinuous L

    Why: Hold the button for 3 to 5 frames at key moments. One will have open eyes and the best expression.

    If it looks wrong: Too many near-duplicates? Use short taps. Missing fast dance moves? Use Continuous Shooting: Hi.Too many near-duplicates? Use short taps. Missing fast dance moves? Use High-speed continuous.Too many near-duplicates? Use short taps. Missing fast dance moves? Use Continuous H.

  • Exposure compensation (+/−)

    0, then adjust in steps of 0.3

    Why: Tells the camera "brighter" or "darker" without leaving Aperture Priority.

    If it looks wrong: Faces too bright against a dark stage? Try −0.7. Person in front of a bright window? Try +0.7 to +1.0.

Flash etiquette

  • Always ask the event lead before using flash.
  • Do not use flash during prayers, arti, or murti darshan unless the event lead explicitly allows it.
  • Flash distracts devotees and flattens the warm light that makes these moments beautiful. Raise the ISO or open the aperture instead.

By situation

Starting points for common moments. Adjust from here based on what you see on the screen.

  • Indoor hall or sabha

    Seated assembly, speakers, kirtan, general coverage indoors.

    Aperture
    f/2.8 to f/4
    Min. shutter
    1/125 s for seated people, 1/250 s for people moving
    ISO
    Auto, up to 12800
    Focus
    Continuous + face/eye detection
    White balance
    Auto
    Drive
    Continuous, low speed

    Tip: Shoot from the sides and back. Never block the audience’s view of the stage.

  • Stage performance

    Dance, drama, and kids’ performances under stage lights.

    Aperture
    As wide as your lens allows (f/2.8 or lower)
    Min. shutter
    1/500 s for dance, 1/250 s for speakers
    ISO
    Auto, up to 12800 (25600 if needed)
    Exposure comp.
    −0.7 (dark stages make the camera overexpose faces)
    Focus
    Continuous + eye detection
    Drive
    Continuous, high speed

    Tip: Stage lights change color fast. Shoot RAW so the color can be corrected later.

  • Low-light arti and diya moments

    Arti, diya lighting, and other moments lit only by flame.

    Aperture
    Widest your lens allows (f/1.8 to f/2.8)
    Min. shutter
    1/125 s (1/60 s if the person is still and you brace yourself)
    ISO
    Auto, up to 25600
    Exposure comp.
    −0.3 to −0.7, so the flames keep their shape
    White balance
    Auto with the ambience option, to keep the warm glow
    Flash
    Off

    Tip: Focus on a lit face, not the flame itself. Tuck your elbows against your body to stay steady.

  • Outdoor daylight (e.g. Walkathon)

    Walking, outdoor games, and anything in sunshine.

    Aperture
    f/5.6 to f/8
    Min. shutter
    1/500 s
    ISO
    Auto (will stay low, around 100 to 400)
    Focus
    Continuous + face detection
    White balance
    Auto or Daylight
    Drive
    Continuous, low speed

    Tip: Keep the sun behind you or to your side. If faces are in shadow, add +0.7 exposure compensation.

  • Group photos

    Volunteers, families, guests, and sant photos.

    Aperture
    f/5.6 to f/8, so every row is sharp
    Shutter
    1/200 s
    ISO
    Auto
    Focus
    On a face in the front third of the group
    Drive
    A short burst of 3 to 5, to beat blinks

    Tip: Stand a little above the group, zoom to about 35 to 50 mm, and count down out loud.
