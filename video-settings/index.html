The basics

Video is many photos per second. A few settings decide how smooth it looks, and they should stay fixed for a whole clip so the look doesn’t change halfway through.

  • Frame rate

    How many pictures per second the video records.

    30 frames per second (30p) looks smooth and natural. 24p looks like a movie. 60p can be slowed down for slow motion.

  • Shutter speed (the 180° rule)

    How long each frame is exposed. In video, it changes how motion looks.

    Set it to about double the frame rate: 1/60 s at 30p, 1/50 s at 24p, 1/125 s at 60p. Faster looks choppy; slower looks smeary.

  • Resolution

    How many pixels each frame has: 4K (3840×2160) or 1080p (1920×1080).

    4K is sharper and lets the editor crop in. It fills cards faster, needs a fast card, and makes the camera warmer.

Rough storage math: every 100 Mbps of bit rate uses about 45 GB per hour. Check the bit rate your camera shows next to the format you choose.

Starting settings

Set these before you press record. Each card says why, and what to change if the footage looks wrong.

  • Frame rate

    30p (29.97)

    Why: Smooth, natural motion for speeches and programs, and it suits U.S. indoor lighting.

    If it looks wrong: Planning slow motion? Record that clip at 60p. Lights flicker at 24p? Go back to 30p.

  • Shutter speed

    1/60 s at 30p · 1/125 s at 60p

    Why: About double the frame rate gives natural-looking motion blur.

    If it looks wrong: Motion looks choppy? The shutter is too fast; lower it. Lights flicker or roll in bands? Use 1/60 s or 1/120 s.

  • Exposure mode

    M (with Exposure Ctrl Type: P/A/S/M Mode), ISO AUTOM (Manual exposure), ISO AutoM (Manual), with auto ISO

    Why: Shutter and aperture stay put so the look stays steady. Auto ISO handles brightness.

    If it looks wrong: Brightness pulses as people walk past? Set a fixed ISO instead of Auto.

  • Resolution and format

    XAVC S 4K (XAVC S HD for 1080p)3840×2160 (4K), 29.97 fps3840×2160; 30p, video file type H.264 8-bit (MP4)

    Why: 4K is sharper and gives the editor room to crop. These formats are easy for most editing software.

    If it looks wrong: Card-speed warning, or the camera is getting hot? Switch to 1080p.

  • Picture profile

    Picture Profile: OffPicture Style: Standard (Canon Log: Off)Set Picture Control: Standard

    Why: Standard looks good straight out of the camera, so clips can be shared without color work.

    If it looks wrong: Footage looks flat and gray? A Log profile is probably on. Turn it off.

  • White balance

    Fixed, not Auto. Indoors: about 3200 to 4000 K. Outdoors: Daylight.

    Why: Auto keeps re-guessing. When someone in orange walks through the frame, the whole clip shifts color.

    If it looks wrong: Footage too orange? Lower the number (for example 4000 K → 3400 K). Too blue? Raise it.

  • Autofocus

    Continuous AF, Face/Eye Prior. in AF: OnMovie Servo AF: Enable, Subject to detect: PeopleAF-F (Full-time AF), AF/MF subject detection: People

    Why: Keeps a speaker or performer sharp as they move, without touching the lens.

    If it looks wrong: Focus hunts or jumps to the background? Switch to manual focus, especially on a tripod, for murti close-ups, or when shooting through a crowd.

  • Audio level

    External mic, loudest sounds peaking around −12 dB

    Why: Viewers forgive soft video but not bad sound. The built-in mic hears the room and your hands.

    If it looks wrong: Sound crackles? It is hitting 0 dB; turn the level down. Too quiet? Move the mic closer or ask the sound team for a mixer feed.

  • Headphones

    Plugged in and listening the whole time

    Why: The level meter can look fine while a mic is unplugged, buzzing, or picking up the air conditioning.

    If it looks wrong: Hear hum or buzz? Check the cable and move away from light dimmers and speakers.

  • Stabilization

    SteadyShot: Standard (Active when walking, Off on a tripod)IS mode: On. Movie digital IS: On when walking, Off on a tripodVibration reduction on. Electronic VR: ON when walking, OFF on a tripod

    Why: Smooths out hand shake. The strongest modes crop the frame slightly.

    If it looks wrong: Footage wobbles or warps on a tripod? Turn stabilization off.

Advanced: Log profiles

Log (Sony S-Log3, Canon Log, Nikon N-Log) records a flat, gray-looking image that keeps more detail in bright and dark areas. It only pays off if someone will color-grade the footage, and exposure is easier to get wrong. Use the standard profile unless your editor asks for Log.

On the a7 IV, S-Log3 is in Picture Profile PP8 and PP9.On the R6 Mark II, use Shooting menu → Canon Log settings → Canon Log: On.On the Z6III, check the manual for N-Log; it needs a 10-bit video file type.

Long recordings: limits, heat, and batteries

Recording-time limits
Some cameras stop a single clip after a set time. The Canon R6 Mark II stops at 6 hours per clip; check your model’s manual. Start a fresh clip at natural breaks so one problem never costs the whole program.
Overheating
4K and 60p make the most heat. For long stage programs, use 4K at 30p or 1080p, keep the camera out of direct sun, and tilt the screen away from the body. If the camera shows a temperature warning, switch to a lower format at the next break.
High temperature setting
Sony (Auto Power OFF Temp.) and Nikon (Auto temperature cutout) offer a High setting that lets the camera run hotter. Use it only on a tripod; the manufacturers warn the body can get hot enough to cause low-temperature burns if handheld.
Batteries
Video drains batteries much faster than photos. Bring at least three charged batteries for a 2 to 3 hour program and swap during breaks, not mid-speech.
Cards
Format cards in the camera before each event, after the previous footage is backed up. Bring a spare card.

By situation

Starting points for common shoots. Adjust from here based on what you see and hear.

  • Stage program

    Speeches, kirtan, dance, and drama from the audience area.

    Support
    Tripod
    Format
    4K 30p (1080p 30p for very long programs)
    Shutter
    1/60 s
    Aperture
    f/2.8 to f/4
    ISO
    Auto, up to 6400
    White balance
    Fixed, about 3200 to 4000 K to match the stage lights
    Focus
    Continuous with face detection; manual on a locked wide shot
    Audio
    A feed from the sound mixer, or a mic near the stage speakers

    Tip: Keep one locked wide shot of the whole stage running start to finish. That is your safety copy.

  • Walking or outdoor event

    Walkathon, processions, and outdoor activities.

    Support
    Handheld, or a gimbal if you have one
    Format
    4K 30p
    Shutter
    1/60 s with an ND filter; without one, up to 1/250 s is fine
    Aperture
    f/5.6 to f/8
    ISO
    As low as it goes
    White balance
    Daylight
    Stabilization
    Strongest setting
    Audio
    Wind filter on, or a mic with a fur windscreen

    Tip: Walk with knees bent and elbows in. Only walk backward with a spotter guiding you.

  • Interviews and testimonials

    One person talking about their experience.

    Support
    Tripod at the person’s eye level
    Format
    4K or 1080p, 30p
    Shutter
    1/60 s
    Aperture
    f/2.8 to f/4, for a soft background
    White balance
    Fixed
    Focus
    Continuous with eye detection, or manual focus on the eyes
    Audio
    Clip-on (lav) mic about a hand’s width below the chin; headphones on

    Tip: Have the person look at you, not the lens. Record 10 seconds of room silence at the end for the editor.

  • B-roll

    Short clips of details and atmosphere to cut over speeches.

    Format
    4K 60p, so it can be slowed down
    Shutter
    1/125 s
    Aperture
    f/2.8 to f/5.6
    Clip length
    5 to 10 seconds, holding still
    White balance
    Fixed for each room

    Tip: Get a wide, a medium, and a close-up of the same thing: diyas, decorations, hands doing seva, faces in the audience.
